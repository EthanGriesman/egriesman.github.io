Workflow for UMI-E+ Data Integration

This project involved developing a scalable Python program to automate the extraction of geometric data from 3D models of urban locations in downtown Des Moines, Iowa. The tool was designed to efficiently process large datasets, capturing detailed information about building layouts, trees, and surrounding urban features. By automating this previously manual task, the program significantly streamlined the workflow for urban energy simulations, enabling more accurate modeling of environmental impacts such as shading and moisture distribution. Over the course of the project, the program generated more than 50,000 new data files containing precise tree parameters, which allowed for detailed analyses of their effects on building energy performance. The work contributed to a deeper understanding of urban energy dynamics and sustainability considerations in city planning. The results were presented at the National Conference for Undergraduate Research in Long Beach, California, highlighting both the technical innovation and practical applications of the project..

Single Cycle MIPS Processor

Designed with MIPS Assembly and VHDL to support arithmetic, logic, and memory instructions. Integrated pipeline stages (IF, ID, EX, MEM, WB) with hazard detection and forwarding units to optimize instruction throughout and resolve data/control hazards. Compared processor performance with non-pipelined implementation, demonstrating a significant reduction in clock cycles.
